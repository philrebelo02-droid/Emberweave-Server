CONFIRMED (a clip Phil approved used it) · FALSIFIED (killed by a test) · MACHINERY (about the tool,

**RULE 8 — CLAUDE WRITES v1 OF EVERY STATE, ALONE. ONE REJECTION HANDS THAT STATE TO THE THREAD UNTIL APPROVAL.**
*"you try every prompt once, if you fail once, you ask the thread to help."* The stage is **per STATE**
and ends at approval. **Claude trying first is not a risk being taken — it is the mechanism**: the

**RULE 9 — CLAUDE JUDGES THE CLIP FIRST: GOOD GOES TO PHIL, BAD IS REFIRED IMMEDIATELY, WITH THE THREAD.**
*"if you think its good ask me, if you think its bad refire right away"* · *"with the threads help"*.
**Phil is not the QA layer** — but *"I could not find a defect"* is not *"this is good"*, and every

**RULE 10 — THE THREAD IS THE PROVEN BETTER PROMPTER UNTIL THE LEDGER SAYS OTHERWISE.**
*"ChatGPT is proven the better prompter until you stop having to go to him from mess ups."* That is a

allowed in exactly one form — a question back to him, with the measurement that prompted it.**

**RULE 11 — WHEN CLAUDE GETS IT WRONG IT GOES TO THE THREAD AS "THE STEPS": WHAT / HOW / WHY / ADVISE.**
Those four headings, those names, that order, with every failed clip attached and labelled.
**Claude does not answer its own Advise heading.**

**RULE 12 — ALWAYS PROBE THE THREAD FOR HOW HE DID IT, NOT JUST FOR THE OUTPUT.**
*"yes always probe chatgpt for notes on how he does things"* — the script, the fitted constants, what

**RULE 13 — NOTHING SKIPS TESTING.** A suggestion from the thread is banked in `04.1 - PROMPT BIBLE LESSONS LEARNED` §5.5, fired on the

**RULE 14 — CLAUDE NEVER MAKES OR EDITS ART, AND WHEN THE FAULT IS IN THE PLATE, ASK THE THREAD TO REMAKE IT.**
*"if chatgpt thinks we need to change the static art, ask him to remake it to match my preference."*
A description of a pose change is a drawing instruction handed to someone who cannot draw.

**RULE 15 — WHEN PHIL POINTS AT SOMETHING, FIND OUT WHICH. NEVER INVENT A NAME FOR IT.**
A label Claude coins becomes a specification downstream — and it gets handed to an image model that
draws to the label instead of to the image.

**RULE 16 — RUN THE CHECKLIST (ch.8) OVER EVERY DRAFT BEFORE IT GOES NEAR THE COMPOSER.**
Then the delivery gate, then `lockcheck.py` at exit 0. **The rules give Claude the pen; they suspend

would this fact get into the prompt?* If the answer is no, the entry is not done — put it in the

## 1.2 The shape

**LOCKS + a short state description.** Never free prose. Locks are copied byte-for-byte from the
last APPROVED prompt for that hero — never retyped from memory, never "improved".

Order, as approved on Vireo (8/8):

1. `Flat pure <#COLOUR> background.` — FIRST, never after the action.
2. **Coverage fact, front-loaded** (women): name the garment, say it covers her, closed and unbroken
   in every frame. This sentence is why the nudity stopped.
3. **Size anchor, early**: proportions exactly those of the supplied image in every frame, never
   growing, swelling or inflating.
4. `<Hero> is full body centered, 3/4 <LEFT|RIGHT>.` · `She has exactly two arms.` · the hands-on-prop
   line ending `and never lets go.`
5. **← THE STATE DESCRIPTION, ≤199 characters.**
6. The appeal sentence, verbatim (women, every state including attack and hit).
7. The physics sentence, verbatim (women).
8. Face lock · `head never turns away` · non-emissive backdrop · `Same costume throughout.` ·
   `Locked-off static camera, no zoom, no pan.` · the settle line (every state except the walk).

## 1.3 The seven non-negotiables — never trimmed to fit the cap

1. `Flat pure #FF00FF magenta background.` (or the hero's actual plate colour) — FIRST.
2. `has exactly two arms` — stops the third arm.
3. `and never lets go` on EVERY held object.
4. `settles into exactly her opening stance on the final frame` — lands the End Frame.
5. `head never turns away`
6. `Locked-off static camera, no zoom, no pan` — and NO other camera vocabulary.
7. `Same costume throughout.`

**When over the cap, cut DESCRIPTION, never a lock.** Proved on 8 Sep: trimming `and never lets go`
and `Same costume throughout` to hit a cap lost a staff for 100 frames and turned a costume into a
different garment — the only two faults in the batch, exactly the two clauses cut.

### The length numbers — UNRESOLVED, and do not invent a safe limit

Four figures are on record and they do not reconcile. All four are kept, with what each one is:

| figure | what it actually refers to | status |
|---|---|---|
| **2000** | the composer's own counter reads `n/2000` | the stated cap |
| **~1040** | a 1122-char PASTE was silently cut at 1042 with a limit toast (9 Sep) — multi-byte characters count more than one | observed once, on the 2.0 dual-plate composer |
| **1650–1850** | the band 152 female prompts ran at with zero lock failures (9 Sep) | observed, and it exceeds the 1040 figure |
| **≤199** | the STATE DESCRIPTION field only, in the locks+description shape (§1.2) — not a whole prompt | a different measurement entirely |

The 1650–1850 band and the ~1040 cutoff cannot both be true of the same input route, so at least one
of them is route-specific: a real keyboard PASTE is what was cut at 1042, while typed text has gone
in at 1,137 and 1,149 intact (11 Sep, Umbris). **The 600–780 figure that appears in older documents
is simply wrong and cost real quality.**

**So: do not invent a universal safe limit, and do not quote one of these at Phil as settled.**
Verify the CURRENT input route each time — count the assembled text mechanically, put it in, read the
box back, and compare. Keep every lock and a complete final sentence whatever the number turns out to
be. **A displayed character counter does not prove a paste arrived intact** (§1.4).

## 1.4 How to write the description

- **Positive description only.** A state prompt says what the hero DOES. The only negations allowed
  are the camera lock and the background lock. Stacking prohibitions produced random spasms — the
  model must still fill six seconds, so removing every trajectory leaves directionless twitching.
- **The EFFECT is the subject of the sentence, not the body.** Six seconds of budget goes to
  whatever the sentences are ABOUT. In a cast, no more than two sentences may have a body part as
  their subject. Approved: *"Dark purple mist kindles against his palm, threaded with deep red heat."*
  Rejected: *"His weight sinks slowly onto his back foot, his shoulders roll back and his chest lifts."*
- **Stillness is the threat — a cast needs a HOLD.** A still figure under a violent effect reads as
  more dangerous AND deforms less. *"the darkness churns faster against his absolute stillness."*
- **"Continuous motion every frame" is FALSE for casts.** It is genuine guidance for IDLE and WALK,
  where a held pose reads as a dead clip; generalised to a cast it forbids the hold and produces a
  fidget. Idle and walk get continuous motion; a cast gets a hold.
- **Adverbs steer INTENT, not joint order.** *"with dreadful certainty"*, *"fingers close
  deliberately"*. Not *"rises unhurried, elbow leading and wrist trailing"*. Both control speed; only
  one makes it that hero instead of a person performing a move.
- **Texture words, unique per ability.** *"crawling folds"*, *"rolling crowns"*, *"ragged burning
  mantle"*. Reused phrasing across a hero's abilities is how a hero ends up with one animation
  wearing three names.
- **One committed arc**: raise → gather → HOLD → commit → return. Spend the middle on the hold and
  the end on one decisive act.
- **Shorter says more.** Approved descriptions run 700–900 chars; rejected ran 900–1200 and said less.
- **Locks are reused verbatim; the DESCRIPTION never is.** Carrying a description between states is
  what makes a hero's abilities look alike.
- **Every arch, bend, swell or reach carries its bound in the same sentence** — how far, what stays
  planted, `head stays above shoulder height`. Unbounded "bends back through her spine" folded Vireo
  in half.
- **⛔ THE UNBOUNDED THING IS THE THING THE MODEL RUNS AWAY WITH. Nerisse attack, 16 Sep 2026, two
  fires, 50 credits — and the second failure is the first one MOVED, not a new problem.**

  | fire | what carried a bound | what did NOT | what the model did with the free thing |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **v1** | the striking arm (*that arm straight*), the head (*holding its height*) | **her legs and stance** | a wide deep lunge, **head-top down 58 px** (control: the approved walk drops 6) |
  | **v2** | the stance (*feet under her hips, no wider than at the start*), the head | **the FORK'S OWN HEIGHT** | the flourish sent the fork to her hip and nothing brought it back, so the strike became a **40 px nudge at waist level** — reach x=212 from a plate start of 252 |

  **v2's crouch fix worked perfectly — 0 px head drop, sqrt-area −3.7%/+0.5%, skirt closed.** Bounding
  the stance did exactly what it was written to do. **The fault did not come back; it relocated to the
  one part of the action still unnamed.**

  **SO §1.4's rule is wider than it reads. It is not only about arches and bends on a BODY.**
  **THE PROP THAT PERFORMS THE ACTION NEEDS ITS OWN BOUND, IN ITS OWN SENTENCE, EVERY TIME —
  HEIGHT, REACH AND END POSITION.** A weapon is not covered by the grip lock (*never lets go* says she
  keeps hold of it, not where she holds it) and it is not covered by a stance bound.

  **AND DO NOT BOUND A REACH BY A FRAME EDGE.** v1 said *"levelling at the LEFT edge of frame"* and
  §1.4's own older line — *"Aim and reach name an EDGE of frame"* — is what put it there. **An edge is
  a DESTINATION, and a standing figure gets to a destination by travelling: it lunged.** Bound the
  reach by the PROP'S OWN geometry against the plate instead — *"sweeping back up to the height it
  starts at and driving out ahead of her"* — which fixes the reach without giving it somewhere to go.
  **The older wording is kept above because it still holds for AIM on a cast that must point
  off-screen; it is wrong for a strike a standing figure performs in place.**

  **AND v3 ADDED THE REFINEMENT, 16 Sep 2026, a third fire:** the fork WAS bounded this time —
  *"the fork sweeping back up to the height it starts at and driving out ahead of her"* — and **she
  raised it overhead and held it there for 70 frames.** Reach x=128, head-top drop 22 px, everything
  else clean, and it is a **salute, not a strike**.

  **⛔ A BOUND MUST NAME A POSITION, NEVER A DIRECTION OF TRAVEL.** *"up to"* is a direction and this
  model executes directions — it took the verb and dropped the target. *"level with her own shoulder,
  the fork ahead of her"* is a position and has nowhere further to go. **Same family as `03 - ART`
  §2.1's banned *"an angle named as a DIRECTION"* and §2.4b's rotation trap: this project keeps paying
  for a verb of motion written where a target was meant.** Check every bound for a travel verb —
  *up, out, back, forward, toward, past, across* — and rewrite it as a place.

  **THE GENERAL TEST, before every fire: list every moving part in the action — body, limbs, stance,
  and each prop — and name the one with no bound. That is what the next failure will be. Then read
  every bound you DID write and check it names a place, not a movement.**

- **⛔ A COUNT IS NOT A BOUND. "ONE strike" HAS FAILED EIGHT TIMES ON ONE STATE.** Nerisse attack,
  16 Sep 2026, v1 through v8, 200 credits. **Every one of the eight carried the strike as a NUMBER** —
  *"2-3s: ONE strike"* — and most also carried a reinforcement: *"Snap to it and STOP: no further
  extension, no slow push"*, *"Never a partial extension"*, *"no second thrust"*.

  **v8 struck SEVEN times** — the fork is extended at 1.10s, 1.61s, 2.15s, 2.70s, 3.80s, 4.35s and
  5.41s of a six-second clip. The count was stated, the repeat was explicitly forbidden, and the model
  did it seven times anyway.

  **Why a count cannot work here.** The model has ~6 seconds to fill and a count says nothing about
  what occupies the other five. A number is an arithmetic fact ABOUT the clip, not a description of
  any moment IN it — there is no frame you can draw that shows "one". Left with unfilled time and a
  pose it likes, it repeats the pose. **This is the unbounded-thing rule again, with TIME as the
  unbounded thing:** the prompt bounded the strike and left 1-2s, 3-4s and 4-5s to invent.

  **The axis to change is giving every second its own occupation**, so there is no room left for a
  second strike — not restating the count, and not adding a ninth prohibition. **MASTER RULE 21: when
  something fails twice the axis changes; this one has failed eight times on a single state.**

  **UNTESTED.** Written from measurement of the eight fires, not from a fire that fixed it. Whoever
  tries it first records the result here.

- **Props drawn on the costume stay attached and in frame.** "taut strings" alone was read as
  stretchable and they became rigid beams leaving the frame.
- **Aim and reach name an EDGE of frame.** "levels that hand at the target" is too vague.
- **A walk needs stride mechanics, not "in place"**: heel-to-toe, alternating legs, rear foot passing
  under and forward, body pinned to the same size and position. The word that works is **treadmill**.
- **Casts must contain a cast.** Write the BODY mechanics of the spell — the draw, the drive, the
  release — not a described visual effect. FX are composited by the game; baked FX smear into the sheet.

## 1.5 When a gesture will not happen — SHOW the model the pose

**THE RULE: when a gesture will not happen, stop rewriting the sentence and give the model the
finished pose as an END FRAME.** Text competes with the plate's implied action; a second plate does
not, because it leaves only the path between two fixed poses to invent. **The return is free** —
`pingpong:true` plays the sheet forward then backward, so the clip needs only the RAISE and the
engine supplies the lower, landing on cell 0, the plate pose exactly.
*(It took fifteen fires to learn — `04.1` **C8**.)*

### 1.5a The end plate is a HARD TARGET — whatever is missing from it gets deleted

**Every feature the hero must keep has to be PRESENT in the end plate. An end plate is not a hint
about the arms; it is a full specification of the finished frame, and anything absent from it is an
instruction to remove that thing.** A drape left out of the end plate collapsed to that plate's own
value inside the first second and stayed there — the model was drawing the plate it was given,
correctly. *(The measured evidence, three identical fires — `04.1` **C10**.)*

### 1.5b Verifying a new end plate — BOTH halves, always

1. Diff the new end plate against the OLD end plate — change confined to the region you asked to
   change; **AND**
2. **Measure the hero's FEATURES against the START plate** — cloth area, shroud, silhouette,
   everything not involved in the gesture — and confirm the numbers match.

**Half 2 is the one that gets skipped, and half 1 alone passes a broken plate:** a diff cannot tell
*"added an arm"* from *"added an arm over something it deleted"*. *(`04.1` **C10a**.)*

### 1.5c An asset that arrives around a correction is not the corrected one
The thread produced a "corrected" file seconds before a "skip that" message landed, and it was used
without diffing. It was a redraw: figure shifted and smaller, arm bent differently, 7.7% of pixels
changed. **Diff any asset that arrives near a correction against what it was meant to preserve.**
The timestamp is not proof of which instruction it followed.

## 1.6 The no-lists

- **FX: no bodies, no figures, no silhouettes, no EFFIGIES.** Every FX prompt carries the four words.
  A spell FX is a clean painted-fantasy magic effect — never a candle-person, doll, mannequin, face
  or limb, however "object-like" the prompt calls it.
- **Spell FX are STYLIZED ANIMATED-GAME VFX, never photoreal.** Bold graphic shapes, cel-shaded
  colour bands, crisp edges, punchy exaggerated motion, flat painted highlights. No simulated liquid,
  no realistic material rendering.
- **Banned in any state description:** `glances back` and `before returning <facing>` (→ a full 360
  spin); any mouth or tongue instruction (→ redraws the face); the words chest / breasts / bust
  (→ open bodice, inflation).
- **Never name a mask or helmet** — naming it invites removal. Write instead: *"his head, face and
  everything on it stay EXACTLY as in the image in every frame; his hands never go near his face."*

|---|---|

### How Hailuo's filter behaved — historical

**THERE ARE TWO DIFFERENT REFUSALS. Know which one you are looking at.**

## 1.10 The five decisions behind every prompt

Every prompt answers these five. If one is unanswered, the prompt is not ready.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Which exact hero, state, source plate, costume and held objects are involved? |
| **Action** | What observable event makes this an attack, cast, walk or reaction? |
| **Sequence** | What happens first, at the peak, and at the end? |
| **Constraints** | What must stay unchanged while that action happens? |

## 1.13 Assemble in four layers

1. **The visual contract** — the actual flat background colour, full-body framing, source
   orientation, fixed size, identity, and the applicable costume/anatomy/prop locks.
2. **The action** — one main action with an understandable sequence: prepare, commit, hold if
   appropriate, then recover or reach the supplied end pose. Visible verbs: *raises, levels, draws,
   thrusts, braces, opens*. Plus one or two character-specific qualities.
   Ordering words that resolve sequence: *first · then · at the apex · only after*. **Timing is a
   requested trajectory, not a guarantee of exact frame timing.**
3. **Preservation during motion** — the approved camera, face, costume, non-emissive background and
   framing locks. Bound every large movement in the action sentence itself.
4. **The correct ending** — same start/end plate: finish in the opening pose. DIFFERENT end plate:
   finish in THAT pose and **do not also demand the opening stance**. If playback is reversed for
   recovery, the clip only needs the outward action.

    [Actual background.] [Identity, orientation, full-body framing, fixed proportions.]
    [Applicable costume, anatomy and held-object locks.] [Unique ordered action.]
    [Applicable secondary motion and face locks.] [Non-emissive backdrop and camera lock.]
    [Ending appropriate to the supplied end plate.]

**This is a structure, not text to paste unchanged.** Resolve every bracket. Never invent a garment
description for a character you have not inspected.

## 1.14 Give each state its own job

- **Idle** — subtle continuous life: breathing, a small weight shift, fabric settling, an approved
  flourish. Never a repeated attack, never a frozen pose.
- **Walk** — a treadmill gait with alternating steps and a stable body position, matched to the
  anatomy (heel-to-toe for a biped; an established hover motion for a flyer). **"In place" alone does
  not explain a gait.** No floor, footprints or contact effects unless requested.
- **Attack** — the strike is unmistakable: the attacking limb or weapon, screen direction, ONE
  committed contact moment, recovery. No long introduction that eats the clip. **It must read with
  all optional FX removed.**
- **Hit** — a readable recoil then recovery, grip and costume preserved. Not automatically a fall,
  spin or counter-attack.
- **Abilities and ultimate** — each gets a different action signature. A cast may need stillness at
  its peak; **continuous motion is not a universal requirement.**
- **Passive** — match the actual requested presentation, subtle loop or discrete activation. **Never
  borrow the idle action and relabel it.**

## 1.15 Make it THIS hero, not any hero

**The movement profile — four choices:** how the hero initiates motion · the path the action follows ·
where the hero pauses · how the hero recovers. Only choices compatible with the visible anatomy and props.

- *disciplined sentinel* — direct economical preparation, aligned weapon path, firm stop, orderly return to guard.
- *void entity* — unnervingly quiet preparation, one deliberate gesture, sustained apex, controlled withdrawal. No athletic footwork if the plate hovers.
- *theatrical summoner* — a presenting gesture, open readable silhouette, deliberate reveal, graceful close.

These are contrasting examples, **not stereotypes to apply**. Phil may want an explosive void entity.
The brief controls the interpretation.

**Personality is shown, not asserted.** Weak: *"She is confident, powerful, mysterious and majestic."*
Stronger: *"She holds the raised staff perfectly still for a beat, then lowers its tip with a small,
decisive motion."* The second is evidence of confidence. Keep the ONE quality that makes the movement
feel intentional; delete the rest.

**THE NAME REMOVAL TEST.** Remove the hero's name and the effect colour from the draft. Would the
action still suggest this character? If it could serve every caster unchanged, revise the initiation,
the rhythm or the recovery. A completely unique joint movement is not required — a recognisable
combination is.

**Originality comes from performance, not from new anatomy**, new floating props, a different
costume or an invented transformation. Prefer a distinctive pause, path or recovery before adding
objects or events.

## 1.16 Differentiate a hero's whole set BEFORE polishing one prompt

Outline every state's action first, then compare the outlines. This is what stops three abilities
becoming one cast with three spell names.

Compare four observable features: **preparation** (where does commitment begin) · **main path**
(rise, press forward, draw inward, sweep across) · **rhythm** (snap, build, hold, recoil) ·
**peak silhouette** (what is visibly different at the most important frame).

As a drafting heuristic, change **at least two** of the four between major abilities. This is a
heuristic, not an acceptance gate — the kit and Phil's intent may justify related gestures.

**Making an ultimate stronger without breaking the image:** increase the contrast between
preparation and release — a longer purposeful hold, a sharper decisive event, a more commanding
silhouette. **Do not reach for camera shake, a zoom, a body enlargement or a full-screen blast.**
Those violate the asset requirements and hide the action.

**Test readability at GAME SIZE, not just full resolution.** A finger motion can be beautifully
generated and invisible in battle. Emphasise an arm, weapon or torso cue when needed, while keeping
the face and costume stable.

## 1.17 Precision and restraint

**Lock what defines success** — the acting limb, the destination, the release order, retained props,
the final pose, frame containment. **Let incidental motion emerge.** Over-directing every joint
creates competing instructions: *"the hand opens only at the apex"* controls a necessary event;
*"the elbow leads, the wrist trails, the shoulder rotates and each finger uncurls in sequence"* adds
complexity without improving it.

**Give every sentence a job.** Label each privately as **identity · action · preservation · ending**,
and for every clause ask: what visible result does this request, or what documented failure does it
prevent? Remove decorative repetition. If a LOCK itself conflicts with the action, resolve the
conflict explicitly — never quietly weaken it.

**Rank what the viewer must notice.** For an attack the strike is primary and the weight shift and
costume response support it; an expression or hip flourish must not take over. For an FX clip the
effect's shape and timing are primary. For an idle it is the recurring life.
**This hierarchy is WHY the female strike exception exists** (§2.5): the secondary physical motion
stays, the posing instructions that compete with the strike come out.

**Positive motion, targeted constraints.** These are different jobs: *"raises the staff in one short
arc"* creates an action; *"never lets go"* protects a prop. **A ban list cannot supply a missing action.**

**Compress in this order:** repeated adjectives and duplicated assurances → abstract prose replaced
by one visible verb and destination → optional flourishes that compete with the state. **Preserve
causal order, the ending and required locks.** A shorter prompt is not automatically a better one.

## 1.18 Diagnose a failure BEFORE rewriting

**First establish what was actually generated.** Read the clip's own prompt and settings. Verify the
intended start and end plates, the model, and the optimization state. **A perfect local prompt cannot
fix an empty submitted prompt, the wrong plate, or a rewritten sequence.**

**Then classify the visible failure:**

| symptom | where to look |
|---|---|
| wrong or absent action | the plate pose vs the end target; does the description give a visible event, or do competing flourishes consume it |
| dropped object / missing garment | BOTH plates and the preservation clause; distinguish an intended release from a prop that must stay held |
| spin or wrong facing | contradictory turns, and the plate orientation. **A matching first and last frame cannot rule out a full rotation in between.** |
| cropping | the widest gesture and the highest reach. **More words cannot create missing canvas.** |
| coloured rim / background change | the RAW clip before keying — distinguish generated lighting from keying damage |
| camera drift / deformation | intermediate frames, not the endpoints. **A crouch is not a size change.** |

**Change the smallest relevant part.** Preserve approved motion while repairing a face instruction.
Preserve approved locks while replacing a weak action. If the missing action is caused by an
unsuitable pose, change the PLATE PLAN instead of cycling through synonyms.

**Test a hypothesis, not a hunch.** Write one sentence — *"I think the early release happens because
the prompt describes opening before the arm reaches the apex"* — then change that clause and retain
the rest. **If both wording and plates change, record both; do not credit the wording.** When the same
failure survives a targeted revision, stop repeating and reassess the INPUTS.

**Inspect against the brief:** a numbered contact sheet at start, ¼, ½, ¾, end **plus the action
peak**; watch playback for timing and gait; inspect the relevant feature at useful resolution — hands
for a release, feet for a walk, costume for continuity.

**Hand-specific inspection (Phil, 24 Sep 2026):** hands are a recurring weakness in Emberweave's art.
Before rewriting a hand prompt, inspect the actual delivered image or the relevant full-resolution
frames. Trace forearm → wrist → one palm → visible digits; distinguish a genuinely fused or extra
finger from an occluded finger that cannot be counted. Check thumb opposition, joint bends, glove
seams and contact with a held object. Name the exact defect location and one change to the pose,
plate or prompt, then re-inspect the corrected artefact at the same hand crop and in the full
composition. A general negative such as “no extra fingers” is not proof of a corrected hand.
The preserved, unapproved GRO-59 image is critique evidence only: it came from a classroom engine
fault, so it does not prove a successful art request. ChatGPT finishes current L5a instruction,
then teaches this hand check before later Visual deepeners, per Phil's priority.

## 1.19 Say which state a thing is actually in

**"Prompt checked", "generation submitted", "clip reviewed" and "approved" are four different
things.** Reserve **"fixed"** for an observed correction in the artefact that will actually be used.
A prompt is not approved because Claude likes it, because a metric passed, or because a guide
contains it. Distinguish **text approved · clip approved · shipped**.

Before returning a prompt:

1. I inspected the correct plate, or explicitly named the missing image.
2. Phil's requested intent is preserved, in-game name and correct state.
3. The action came from his brief and the plate — not copied from another state.

6. Anatomy, occupied hands, intended releases and retained props are consistent.

## 1.22 Practice — these cost no credits

1. **Find the contradiction.** A two-armed mage holds a staff and an orb; the draft tells her to
   trail a hand along her hip, release the orb, and never let go of either. Her end plate shows the
   orb still held. *(Three conflicts: hip gesture vs occupied hands; release vs permanent-grip;
   release vs the end target.)*
2. **Differentiate three abilities** for one grounded caster with a retained staff and a free hand,
   using no effect colours or ability names. *(Pass: distinguishable by path, rhythm or peak
   silhouette. Fail: three "raises hand, gathers power, releases" with different adjectives.)*
3. **Repair without redesigning.** The motion is approved; the face changes halfway. *(Pass: motion
   verbatim, only the face clause changes. Fail: a new pose or camera instruction hides the face.)*
4. **Choose the cause.** Three prompts produce the same missing chest panel, and the end plate also
   lacks it. *(Answer: fix the end plate through the art workflow. More costume adjectives cannot
   repair an incorrect target image.)*

**Review rubric**, scored 0/1/2 as a coaching aid only: readability · character fit · distinctiveness ·
economy · feasibility. Revise any zero; for a one, name the precise weakness. **Do not chase a perfect
score by adding detail.**

## 2.6 The face rule — what it actually means

"Her FACE stays exactly as in the image" means her **STRUCTURE** — the same person, the same
features, her eyes as drawn, her natural lip colour as drawn. **She CAN smile, wink, glance or turn
her eyes toward camera.** What fails is a transformation: eyes squeezed shut into a laughing cartoon
grin, a wide open mouth, bright red lipstick appearing, a different face.

The fix that worked is a positive face sentence up front — eyes open in the plate's own shape and
colour, lips keep their natural pale drawn colour, she may smile, her face keeps the plate's own
features in every frame. **NEVER change an approved movement to fix a face. Fix the face sentence only.**

Never write `heavy-lidded`, `leer`, `stare` or `sly` — those produced creepy faces. And **never add
expression direction on top of the block**: Phil drew each woman's face with its own character, and a
run that stacked a smile line on top came out cartoonish across the board.

# MESHY TEXT-TO-MOTION — A DIFFERENT PROMPT LANGUAGE FROM EVERY OTHER TOOL (19 Sep 2026 · HISTORY - 3D pilot closed)

**Four flowery motion prompts in a row were rejected by Phil** (*"comes off as more of a gay guy"*, *"both walks you made were unacceptable"*). The cause was prompting a MOTION model the way we prompt an IMAGE model. Research, 19 Sep:

- **Meshy publishes no prompting guide for motion** — their guide covers text-to-3D only. The "Subject + Material + Style" formula does NOT apply.
- These models are trained on **HumanML3D**: plain captions of about **7–15 words** ("a person walks forward and turns around"). A 330-character paragraph is outside that distribution, so the model falls back to a generic walk.
- **Mood adjectives are the weakest possible input.** Current research (SnapMoGen, 2507.09122) exists because these models "struggle when users employ descriptive vocabulary beyond training captions" and cannot separate a stylistic modifier from a body-part movement.
- **Stylised walking is a known failure case** for text alone: SMooDi, LoRA-MDM and the hypernetwork-LoRA work (2605.13333) all use a **style reference**, not words.
- **What does work:** mechanical, body-part phrasing, the MotionScript style ("places each foot in front of the other", "shifts weight from one hip to the other").

## THE RULES FOR A MESHY MOTION PROMPT
1. **10–25 words. Aim for about 130 characters, not 400.**
2. **Third person, generic subject: "A woman…"** — never a hero name, never "she" with backstory.
3. **One continuous action**, described as mechanics: feet, hips, weight, shoulders, arms.
4. **No mood words.** No sexy, seductive, provocative, flirty, confident, sultry.
5. **BLOCK 1 FEMALE DOES NOT GO IN A MOTION PROMPT.** It is an image and video block. Its behaviour sentence has no mechanical content for a motion model, and it eats half the budget.
6. **Constrain a held weapon in the prompt**, or rigid geometry clips through the body: *"her right arm hanging straight down and held out away from her body."*
7. If words still fail, use a **style reference** — Meshy's Video to Motion — not more words.
